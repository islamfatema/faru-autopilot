Why Chili Peppers Feel Hot (It's Not Actual Heat!)

Published 2026-10-01 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You probably believe chili peppers are actually hot. But it's a clever trick on your brain. There is zero actual thermal heat from chilies. A chemical called capsaicin causes the sensation. It activates TRPV1 pain receptors in your mouth. These receptors usually detect physical heat above 43°C. This means milk soothes by washing capsaicin away. Water doesn't help as capsaicin is oil-soluble. And explains why touching eyes after is painful. Are you brave enough for a ghost pepper?
