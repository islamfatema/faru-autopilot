Why Onions Make You Cry When Cut

Published 2026-09-27 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

Everyone knows cutting onions makes your eyes water uncontrollably. It's a frustrating but seemingly unavoidable part of cooking. But it's not just simple irritation from the raw fumes. The tears are triggered by a specific chemical defense mechanism. It's a deliberate reaction designed to protect the plant. When you slice, cell walls rupture, releasing sulfenic acids and enzymes. These quickly mix and form a gas called syn-propanethial-S-oxide. This compound travels to your eyes, reacting with moisture instantly. This creates a mild sulfuric acid, causing that stinging. Knowing this, you can try cutting under running water or chilling. Or even light a candle nearby to burn off the gas. Is that tear-free onion really worth the extra effort?
