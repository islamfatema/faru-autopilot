Why We Say 'Bless You' After a Sneeze

Published 2026-10-15 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You think saying bless you is just polite manners. Medieval Europeans viewed sudden sneezes with deep fear. In the sixth century, a deadly plague struck Rome. Pope Gregory ordered quick prayers for the sick. This stopped immediate social panic during viral outbreaks. Citizens whispered blessings to ward off death. The religious custom spread rapidly across Western culture. Modern children learn this habit without knowing why. Every cold season triggers this medieval reflex response. Social etiquette demands immediate verbal acknowledgment. You offer well wishes for minor biological reflexes. Today you repeat this ancient panic response. Should we finally drop this medieval health superstition?
