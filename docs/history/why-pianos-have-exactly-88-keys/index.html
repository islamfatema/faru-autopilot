Why Pianos Have Exactly 88 Keys

Published 2026-10-26 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You assume all pianos always had 88 keys. That number seems to be a universal standard. But earlier pianos actually had fewer notes. Often as few as only 60 keys in total. This limited composers and musical scope greatly. As music grew complex, more keys were then added. In the late 1880s, Steinway set the 88-key standard. This covers the full range of human audible hearing. This ensures any composition sounds complete today. It allows composers vast expressive capabilities. This historic choice still shapes music you enjoy. Would music be less rich without 88 keys today? Does this exact number restrict future music?
