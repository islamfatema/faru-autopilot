Why the New Year Starts on January 1st

Published 2026-09-23 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

New Year's Day on January 1st feels ancient. It seems like the natural start to any year. But this specific date is a human choice. Many cultures historically started their year differently. It's not a universal or natural phenomenon. Julius Caesar established January 1st in 45 BCE. He reformed the Roman calendar to align with the sun. January honored Janus, the god of beginnings. Without Caesar's reform, our year might start in March. Imagine spring being the true turning point. Our holidays and school years built on this choice. Should the New Year start with a new season?
