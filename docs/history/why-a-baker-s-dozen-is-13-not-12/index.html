Why a Baker's Dozen is 13, Not 12

Published 2026-10-04 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

Everyone knows a baker's dozen means exactly 13. This tradition seems like a generous offering, right? But its surprising origin isn't just a kind gesture. Its origins are rooted in medieval fear, not kindness. Bakers added extra to avoid very harsh punishments. English medieval law, the Assize of Bread and Ale, from 1266, strictly punished any underweight loaves sold. Flogging or steep fines awaited those who cheated. So, that thirteenth loaf was a legal safeguard. It prevented severe punishment, ensuring their very survival. The baker's 'generosity' was purely economic self-preservation.
