Ancient Rome Had Apartment Blocks - and a Height Limit

Published 2026-09-12 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

Most people in ancient Rome lived in apartments. Blocks called insulae: shops on the ground floor, families stacked above. Built cheap and tall, they kept collapsing and burning. So Augustus set a height limit: 70 Roman feet, about 21 meters. The top floors, hardest to escape, were the cheapest. In Rome, the penthouse was the budget option. Top floor or ground floor - where would you live in Rome? number 3: the first recorded workers' strike in history. Guess what it was about.
