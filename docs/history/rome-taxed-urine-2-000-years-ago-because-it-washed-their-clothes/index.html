Rome Taxed Urine 2,000 Years Ago - Because It Washed Their Clothes

Published 2026-09-20 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

Rome taxed urine - because it washed their clothes. Roman laundries soaked cloth in stale urine. The ammonia in it lifted out dirt and grease. Shops set out pots in the street for people to fill, and the laundries collected them. Emperor Vespasian put a tax on it. When his son complained, Vespasian held a coin to his nose and asked if it smelled. Two thousand years later, 'money doesn't smell' is still a saying.
