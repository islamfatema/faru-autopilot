Ancient Romans Made Plywood Before Modern Industry

Published 2026-11-01 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You think plywood is a modern industrial factory invention. Layering thin sheets of wood feels like 20th-century tech. You were taught early builders only used solid heavy timber. But solid wood warps in damp, cracking expensive furniture. Roman craftsmen solved this over two thousand years ago. Pliny recorded it in 77 AD. They glued thin wood layers with alternating grain paths. Using animal resins, they made panels stronger than raw wood. Look at any desk edge today. Try pressing your thumbnail into layered engineered wood. You are touching a technique invented in imperial Rome. Next episode: Roman glass. What else did Rome build first?
