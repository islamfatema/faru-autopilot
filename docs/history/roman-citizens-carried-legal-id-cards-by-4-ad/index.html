Roman Citizens Carried Legal ID Cards by 4 AD

Published 2026-10-24 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You assume carrying legal ID is a modern government rule. You think state birth records began with modern taxes. And ancient people lived without any official ID cards. That belief is historically false. By the year four AD, Rome required registration. Parents registered newborns within thirty days. Citizens received wooden tablets sealed with wax stamps. They carried these portable IDs to prove citizen status. Without one, you could be taxed as a foreigner. Try showing your modern ID without opening your wallet.
