Ancient Romans Used Bronze Scrapers After Baths

Published 2026-10-23 · Watch on YouTube

What the video says

You picture Roman bathhouses using liquid soap and towels. That common mental image is completely incorrect. Before fluffy towels existed, bathers coated skin in oil. They scraped the dirty oil away with a curved metal tool. The bronze strigil pulled sweat and grime off the body. Skin was left completely clean without any soap at all. Try rubbing lotion on your arm and wiping it with a card. Feel how friction strips away the surface layer cleanly. How did personal hygiene survive centuries without cleansers?
